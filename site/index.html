SELF-DRIVING CARS / FEDERAL CRASH REPORTS

Double-check self‑drivingcar crash reports.

An AI reads the written story in each self-driving car crash report and fills in four checkbox answers, quoting a line as proof. Where the story gave the answer, it matched what the company filed 81.2% of the time, versus 40.6% for always guessing the most common answer.

Try the demoSee the results

THE PROBLEM

The checkboxes do not always match the story.

Companies running self-driving cars must report every crash to the federal auto-safety agency (NHTSA). Each report has a written story plus checkbox answers: what the car hit, the type of road, what the other vehicle was doing, and how badly anyone was hurt.

Anyone who counts crashes by type relies on those checkboxes. When a checkbox disagrees with the story, one of them is wrong. Example: in 81 reports, the company filed “street” where the story describes an intersection.

This project has an AI read each story and fill in those four answers, but only when it can quote a line as proof. Otherwise it says not stated instead of guessing. Then it flags every mismatch for a person to review.

WHAT YOU GET

Every answer comes with its proof.

The AI may only choose from the agency's official answer list, so its answers line up with the filing. Simple code then checks that every quote appears word for word in the story, which stops the AI from inventing proof. It never assumes the company's checkbox is always right.

RESULTS ON 444 REAL REPORTS

Answering only with proof beats guessing.

The AI found 1,196 answers stated in the stories. On those, it matched what the company filed 81.2% of the time, versus 40.6% for always guessing the most common answer (higher is better). Everywhere else it said “not stated”. 99.9% of its quotes were found word for word in the story.

FLAGGED FOR A PERSON TO REVIEW

A mismatch is a question, not a verdict.

The AI flagged 225 answers that differ from what the company filed. The most common one (81 times): the company filed “street” where the story describes an intersection. Either side can be wrong, so a person decides.

For roadway type and injury severity, the AI only ties or barely beats always guessing the most common answer. One answer dominates each of those checkboxes, and stories rarely mention property damage.

The examples below are the companies' own words. In them, AV means the self-driving car and ADS (automated driving system) means its driving software.

HOW IT WORKS

The AI reads. Plain code checks its work.

An AI model (Claude) does the reading. Simple code does the checking: the answer list, the quotes and the scoring.

  1. Collect

    Download the agency's public crash reports and keep each story with the answers the company filed.

  2. Read

    Ask the AI for the four answers, each with one line quoted from the story as proof.

  3. Check

    Throw out any answer that is not on the official list, and any quote not found word for word in the story.

  4. Compare

    Score each answer against the company's filing, and against two simple methods: always guessing the most common answer, and a keyword match.

  5. Flag

    Send up to 50 mismatches to a person to review, instead of assuming the company's answer is always right.

FOR ENGINEERS

Technical details

Data. NHTSA Standing General Order 2021-01 ADS incident CSV (US government, public domain), 1,478 narrative rows parsed on 2026-10-12. Test split: newest 30% by report submission month plus every report from the smallest reporting entity (Gatik AI Inc.), 444 reports. Waymo is 82% of the source rows.

Extraction. Claude Haiku 5.5 via the Claude Code CLI, cached in batches. Four enumerated fields (crash_partner, roadway_type, other_party_movement, injury_severity), each with a verbatim evidence span or "not stated". Pydantic rejects unknown labels and extra keys, and evidence must be an exact substring of the narrative. Valid output 99.5%, evidence exact rate 99.9%, 3.78 s per report, $0.00 cash cost per 1,000 reports.

Metrics. Coverage 67.3% (1,196 of 1,776 slots answered). Answered-slot exact accuracy 81.2% vs 40.6% majority and 42.0% keyword baseline on the same slots. All-row macro accuracy, with abstentions scored wrong: 55.7% vs 54.3% majority and 29.1% keyword. The majority value is computed on the test rows, which favors the baseline.

Review queue. 225 answered mismatches across 68 filed/model pairs, 50 listed with examples. Abstentions count toward coverage, not disagreements. Filed fields are an answer key with label noise, not ground truth, and no hand check is claimed.

Live box. Optional BYOK: the browser calls claude-3-5-haiku-latest or gpt-4o-mini directly with a key held in sessionStorage. Its raw output is shown unvalidated and is not part of the recorded metrics.

Limitations. Waymo dominates the source, so results may not generalize across manufacturers. Roadway type and injury severity barely beat or tie majority because one filed category dominates each field. Narratives are redacted ([XXX]) and rarely state property damage.

See the code and the limits.

The code, the saved AI answers, the tests and the full list of flagged mismatches are on GitHub. Two limits: Waymo filed 82% of the reports in the source data, and the companies' own checkboxes are sometimes wrong.
